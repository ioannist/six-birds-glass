\title{GT6 pressure: valid limit bounds and the conditioning boundary}
\author{}\date{}\maketitle

Let $K$ be the finite wall-up East kernel on $\{0,1\}^8$ at $\epsilon=2/5$,
$e$ its spin-count energy and $L_s(x,y)=K(x,y)\exp(se(y))$ with $s=1/5$.
The submultiplicative row norm
$\Phi_n=\max_x(L_s^n\mathbf1)(x)$ gives
$P=\lim_n n^{-1}\log\Phi_n=\inf_n n^{-1}\log\Phi_n$ by Fekete.
The endpoint of a finite ladder is an upper bound, not the limiting pressure.
Nor does a small difference of successive endpoints certify closeness to the
limit without an additional bound.

\paragraph{Constructive comparison bounds.}
For any strictly positive vector $v$, suppose $av\leq L_sv\leq bv$ with
$a>0$. Positivity gives $a^nv\leq L_s^nv\leq b^nv$. Put
$m=\min_xv(x)>0$ and $M=\max_xv(x)$. Comparing $\mathbf1$ to $v$ gives
$(m/M)a^n\leq\Phi_n\leq(M/m)b^n$, hence $\log a\leq P\leq\log b$.
In the certificate, float iteration merely chooses $v$, which is then
converted to exact binary rational coordinates. Exact kernel probabilities
and outward rational exponential enclosures bound every ratio
$(L_sv)(x)/v(x)$. Rational logarithm enclosures complete the proof. No
convergence assertion about the float iteration is needed. The resulting
bound is approximately $1.039322126141286\leq P\leq1.039617636994712$.

For $0\leq r\leq1$, the Taylor polynomial through degree $m$ is a lower
bound for $\exp r$. Its tail is at most
$\frac{r^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}(1-r/(m+2))^{-1}$. Repeated halving of a positive
argument followed by interval squaring gives general positive arguments;
negative arguments use reciprocal intervals. All rounding is outward.

\paragraph{Initial-law conditioning has zero limiting gap.}
The exact source graph is irreducible and every state has a positive
self-loop. Therefore some power of $L_s$ is strictly positive. For any
nonzero nonnegative row law $\mu$, choose $q$ such that $\nu=\mu L_s^q$
is strictly positive. Then
\[
 (\min_x\nu(x))\Phi_n\leq\nu L_s^n\mathbf1
 \leq(\sum_x\nu(x))\Phi_n.
\]
Taking logs and dividing by $n$ proves that every such initial law has the
same limiting pressure $P$. A fixed shift by $q$ does not affect the limit.
In particular, conditioning a full-support initial law on any nonempty
energy fiber, while retaining the original dynamics, gives this same $P$.
Consequently $P-\sum_f w_fP_f=0$ for any probability weights on those
initial-law conditional profiles. Predictive strictness cannot by itself
supply a positive gap for this choice of limiting conditional pressure.

\paragraph{The old operation is a killed process.}
Zeroing every transfer entry outside one fiber replaces $L_s$ by a principal
restriction, killing trajectories as soon as they exit. This changes the
experiment. In a one-step heat-bath spin kernel every transition inside an
energy fiber is diagonal. Its surviving growth rate is therefore
$\max_{x\in f}K(x,x)\exp(se(x))$. This is a survival-pressure diagnostic;
it is not the initial-law conditioning operation above. A positive weighted
contrast for this killed operation does not discharge the conditional
disintegration or persistent-packaging premises imported from paper [C].
The certificate separately computes the true limiting survival contrast:
writing $a_f=\max_{x\in f}K(x,x)$, it encloses each pressure as
$se(f)+\log a_f$ and sums with exact Gibbs fiber weights. Comparison with
the certified original-process limit gives a diagnostic gap in approximately
$[0.679497405992839,0.679792916846265]$, hence strictly greater than $1/2$.
This preserves a positive limiting diagnostic with its actual killed-path
semantics; it does not turn it into conditional disintegration.

\paragraph{A lawful finite-time alternative.}
Set $Z_n=L_s^n\mathbf1$, use the stationary comparison law $\pi=\pi_{2/5}$,
and let $w_f=\pi(f)>0$. Define
\[
 P_n=\frac1n\log(\pi Z_n),\qquad
 P_{n,f}=\frac1n\log(\pi(\cdot\mid f)Z_n),\qquad
 \Delta_n=P_n-\sum_f w_fP_{n,f}.
\]
These use the original dynamics throughout. Since
$\pi Z_n=\sum_fw_f\pi(\cdot\mid f)Z_n$, concavity of $\log$ proves
$\Delta_n\geq0$. Exact exponential intervals propagated through the finite
matrix powers and exact logarithm intervals certify
$\Delta_n\geq1/1000$ for every $n=1,\ldots,8$ in the frozen N8 comparison.
For example, $\Delta_1\simeq0.030568517668562$ and
$\Delta_8\simeq0.189146816910146$. This finite Jensen contrast is already
present for the stationary comparison law, so it is not a glass-specific
strictness witness. It tends to zero in the infinite-time limit above.
The new certificate records both results rather than identifying them.
