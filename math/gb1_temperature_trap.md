# GB1: Temperature-Protocol Trap Boundary Term

## Setting

Let `X` be finite and let `Phi = {0, ..., m-1}` be a finite clock/phase set.
Phase `phi` carries a declared temperature `T_phi` and a phase-local Gibbs law

```text
pi_phi(x) = exp(-E(x) / T_phi) / Z_phi.
```

The random-scan lift on `Z = X x Phi` is

```text
P((x,phi),(x',phi'))
 = alpha * 1{x'=x} * S(phi,phi')
   + (1-alpha) * 1{phi'=phi} * K_phi(x,x').
```

Assume `0 < alpha < 1`, all Gibbs laws and `s` have full support, and the
clock has bidirected positive edge support (otherwise EPR may be infinite).
The clock kernel `S` has stationary law `s`, and each `K_phi` is reversible
with respect to its own `pi_phi`. The phase Gibbs laws need not coincide.

## Where The Published Zero-EPR Argument Breaks

For the natural candidate measure

```text
hat_mu(x,phi) = pi_phi(x) * s(phi),
```

state edges at fixed `phi` cancel because `K_phi` is reversible with respect to
`pi_phi`. Phase edges at fixed `x` do not cancel unless all `pi_phi` agree. A
phase switch from `phi` to `phi'` contributes the defect
`log(pi_phi(x) / pi_phi'(x))`. Thus the published common-pi protocol-trap
theorem does not apply to temperature schedules with genuinely different Gibbs
laws.

The mixed-square affinity around the elementary cycle is

```text
(E(x') - E(x)) * (1/T_phi - 1/T_phi'),
```

which is nonzero in the non-null regime `Delta E * Delta beta != 0`.

## Proven Identity: Pseudo-EPR At The Candidate Measure

Let the directed edge-flux EPR functional be

```text
EPR(P,mu) = sum_{i,j} mu(i) P(i,j)
              log( mu(i) P(i,j) / (mu(j) P(j,i)) ).
```

Then

```text
EPR(P,hat_mu)
 = alpha * EPR(S,s)
   + alpha * sum_{phi != phi'} s(phi) S(phi,phi') KL(pi_phi || pi_phi').
```

### Proof

Split the directed edge sum into state-update edges and clock-update edges.

For a state edge at fixed phase `phi`,

```text
hat_mu(x,phi) (1-alpha) K_phi(x,x')
  = s(phi) pi_phi(x) (1-alpha) K_phi(x,x').
```

The reverse flux is

```text
s(phi) pi_phi(x') (1-alpha) K_phi(x',x).
```

These are equal by reversibility of `K_phi` with respect to `pi_phi`, so every
state-edge log ratio is zero.

For a clock edge at fixed state `x`,

```text
hat_mu(x,phi) alpha S(phi,phi')
  = alpha pi_phi(x) s(phi) S(phi,phi').
```

The reverse flux is

```text
alpha pi_phi'(x) s(phi') S(phi',phi).
```

The log ratio splits as

```text
log(s(phi)S(phi,phi') / (s(phi')S(phi',phi)))
  + log(pi_phi(x) / pi_phi'(x)).
```

Summing the first term over `x` gives the clock EPR contribution because
`sum_x pi_phi(x) = 1`. Summing the second term over `x` gives
`KL(pi_phi || pi_phi')`. Multiplying by the edge weight
`alpha s(phi)S(phi,phi')` and summing over directed phase edges proves the
identity.

If all `pi_phi` coincide, the KL boundary term is zero. If the clock is also
reversible, `EPR(S,s)=0`, recovering the published zero-EPR conclusion.

## Stationary Repair: Identity and Computable Upper Budget

The uncorrected candidate-measure expression is **not** a general stationary
upper bound. `gb1_stationary_counterexample.json`, regenerated by
`experiments/audit_gb1_stationary_bound.py`, certifies a counterexample with
`X={0,1,2}`, `E=(0,1,2)`, `epsilon=exp(-beta)` equal to
`(4/25,4/7,1/60)`, a reversible clock, `alpha=1/5`, and three reversible
Metropolis kernels. All probabilities and the stationary law are rational.
Positive remainder bounds for the logarithm series certify

```text
EPR(P,mu*) - EPR(P,hat_mu) > 9.5 * 10^-8.
```

The stationary EPR is about `4.85976e-6`, whereas the candidate expression is
about `4.76438e-6`. This invalidates the proposed inequality even for Gibbs
laws with a common energy function and without clock drive. It does not
invalidate the candidate-measure identity above.

Here is a general repair, retaining a computable budget and the common-pi
recovery. Define

```text
b(x,phi) = alpha * sum_phi' S(phi,phi') log(pi_phi(x)/pi_phi'(x)).
r = TV(hat_mu P, hat_mu).
D_k = max_i,j TV(P^k(i,.), P^k(j,.)).
osc(b) = max_z b(z) - min_z b(z).
```

Suppose the lift is irreducible and choose a positive integer `k` with
`D_k < 1`. Such a `k` exists for an irreducible aperiodic finite lift; if all
self-loop probabilities are positive, `k=|X x Phi|-1` works for a carrier
with at least two states, since every ordered pair has a simple path of
length at most `k`, which can be padded by self-loops. For a singleton use
`k=1`. Then its unique stationary law satisfies

```text
EPR(P,mu*) = alpha EPR(S,s) + E_mu*[b]
          = EPR(P,hat_mu) + (mu* - hat_mu)[b],

EPR(P,mu*) <= alpha EPR(S,s)
             + alpha sum_phi,phi' s(phi)S(phi,phi') KL(pi_phi || pi_phi')
             + osc(b) * k*r/(1-D_k).
```

Every constant is a finite computation from the declared kernels and Gibbs
laws, and logarithms can be enclosed by rational intervals. A trivial exact
upper bound is also `alpha EPR(S,s) + max_z b(z)`; the corrected budget above
preserves the sharper candidate term and supplies a controlled correction.

### Proof of the stationary identity

Set `psi(x,phi)=log(s(phi)pi_phi(x))`. On state edges, detailed balance gives
`log(P(z,z')/P(z',z))=psi(z')-psi(z)`. On clock edges it gives

```text
log(P(z,z')/P(z',z))
 = psi(z')-psi(z)
   + log(s(phi)S(phi,phi')/(s(phi')S(phi',phi)))
   + log(pi_phi(x)/pi_phi'(x)).
```

Self-edges contribute zero, so the overlap of the two updates on the diagonal
does not affect this decomposition. In the stationary expectation,
`psi(z')-psi(z)` telescopes to zero. The `log(mu*(z)/mu*(z'))` part of the
edge-flux EPR likewise averages to zero. The phase marginal of `mu*` is `s`:
the marginal evolves by `alpha S + (1-alpha)I`, and the irreducible lift
implies an irreducible phase clock. This proves the first identity. Subtract
the already-proved candidate expression to get the second.

### Proof of the correction

The Dobrushin contraction estimate is
`TV(nu P^k,nu' P^k) <= D_k TV(nu,nu')`. In the finite case it follows by
writing the equal-total signed difference as its positive and negative
parts, each of mass `TV(nu,nu')`, and expressing their output difference as a
mixture of row differences. Also, stochastic TV contraction and a telescoping
sum give `TV(hat_mu P^k,hat_mu) <= k*r`. Hence

```text
TV(mu*,hat_mu) <= D_k TV(mu*,hat_mu) + k*r
              <= k*r/(1-D_k) after rearrangement.
```

A signed measure of total mass zero obeys
`abs((mu*-hat_mu)[b]) <= osc(b) TV(mu*,hat_mu)`, proving the budget.
In the common-pi case `hat_mu` is stationary, so `r=0` and `b=0`; for a
reversible clock this recovers zero stationary EPR exactly. For a smooth
small-temperature-variation family around a fixed irreducible aperiodic
common-pi lift, `r=O(Delta beta)` and `osc(b)=O(Delta beta)`, while
`1-D_k` stays bounded away from zero for a fixed suitable `k`. The correction
and the KL boundary are therefore both `O(Delta beta^2)` on that fixed
family. This does not assert constants uniform over degenerating kernels.

For stationary path-reversal KL, `Sigma_T(mu*) = T EPR(P,mu*)`: expand the
path log ratio and use stationarity at every step, including the vanishing
expected endpoint log ratio. Thus the corrected per-step budget gives the
same finite-horizon stationary budget requested in the design.

## Transient and Deterministic-Schedule Boundaries

No bound of the uncorrected form `Sigma_T(rho) <= T EPR(P,hat_mu)` holds for
arbitrary initial laws. Even with a common pi and no phase change, take the
two-state resampling kernel with uniform pi and `rho=(3/4,1/4)`. At horizon
one the path reversal KL is `log(3)/4 > 0`, although the stationary budget is
zero. The same counterexample lies inside the `0<alpha<1` random-scan class:
use a singleton clock, `alpha=1/3`, and the uniform resampling state kernel;
then the lift has off-diagonal entries `1/3`, and `Sigma_1(rho)=log(3)/6`.
A transient theorem must retain initial/endpoint information costs or
compare with a differently declared reverse experiment. The stationary
repair above is not a transient theorem.

A deterministic successor phase clock on a cycle of length at least three
has one-way support and infinite stationary path-reversal KL under the same
reversal convention. A two-phase successor is a bidirected swap, so the
one-way assertion does **not** apply to that case. A finite scheduled process
compared against a separately declared reversed schedule is a different
path-space experiment and is not covered by this stationary theorem.

## Three-State Demonstration

The implemented demonstration uses `X={0,1,2}`, energies `E=(0,1,2)`, two
temperatures, full heat-bath resampling kernels reversible to each phase Gibbs
law, a reversible two-state clock, and `alpha=1/3`.

In the common-temperature recovery case, all `pi_phi` coincide. The boundary
term is numerically zero and the true lifted-chain stationary EPR is zero to
machine precision.

In the hot/cold case, the true stationary law is computed by power iteration.
The finite path KL `Sigma_T(mu*)` is enumerated exhaustively in floating arithmetic over all lifted paths for
`T=1,2,3` and compared against `T` times the pseudo-EPR boundary. In this
declared three-state example the bound holds for all three horizons. The
mixed-square cycle affinity computed from energy matches the direct sum of
kernel log-ratios, confirming the `Delta E * Delta beta` diagnosis.
