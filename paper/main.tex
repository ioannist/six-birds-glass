\begin{abstract}
A glass and its equilibrium liquid can agree in every routinely monitored quantity and still
evolve differently. Kovacs's 1963 crossover experiment makes the point vivid: at the moment an
aged sample's volume equals its equilibrium value, the volume moves away again. We study the
question behind this on finite Markov models small enough to settle it by exact computation (the
East and Fredrickson--Andersen kinetically constrained spin chains and a Bouchaud-type trap
family):
is a declared readout, here the energy, a \emph{closed} description, one whose present value fixes
its own future statistics? On the declared East scopes where we certify it, it is not, and
we certify this in several independent ways. For an aging East chain, the energy readout's closure deficit
is at least $3/2$ times its constrained-equilibrium value at every tested horizon, certified in
exact rational arithmetic, and the equilibrium value is itself provably nonzero. The Kovacs
protocol yields two preparations with the same mean energy now and different mean energies
later. Two randomized stops of one aging run share the \emph{entire} energy distribution, exactly,
yet their mean energies separate at each of 21 later horizons, so no function of the current
energy law, a fictive-temperature-like index included, predicts the future on that shell. All
$512$ yes/no functions of the energy agree on a further, constructed pair that one structural
coordinate separates. A kill-test panel rejects five look-alike regimes, and one added coordinate resolves
each exhibited pair. Aging constants frozen on East and scored mechanically on FA pass 8 of 22
predictions; the trap column is empty for a reason we prove. Lean checks the abstract theorems
and the exported witness tables. We also report what does not hold: the fixed-point route to a
full strict-extension filing fails here, and a positive infinite-time conditional pressure gap
is provably zero. Every claim is pinned by content hash to its evidence, and every nonclaim is
printed.
\end{abstract}
