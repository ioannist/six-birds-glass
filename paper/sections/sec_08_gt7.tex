\section{GT7: Aging Constants Are Run-Only: Freeze, Transfer, and the Scoreboard}
\label{sec:gt7}

\subsection{The protocol}

GT7 asks whether the numbers that characterize aging on East (the Kovacs crossing time, the peak
time, the hump height, and three others) carry over to a different model without any fitting. The
protocol follows the logic of a pre-registered experiment \cite{nosek-et-al-2018,munafo-et-al-2017}:
fix the readouts, compute the East reference, commit predictions for the target models, and only
then run the targets and score them with a frozen script. Precisely:

\claimref{GT7}{4905b013624462549b9a4cdb18f51d0220f70be29976be461e5a8681978dcab8}
GT7's claim is a discipline plus its unedited outcome. The protocol has five stages, each frozen
by an sha256 content hash:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{F1, readouts freeze.} Six declared readouts (Kovacs $t_K$, $t_{\mathrm{peak}}$,
    hump height, memory-dimension profile, deficit-decay slope, cross-validation checks), frozen in
    a registry with content hash and freeze note.
  \item \textbf{F2, East reference.} The reference surface on East: 56 exact hump-surface cells
    at $N=10$, an $N=16$ Monte Carlo spot check, the closure-deficit ladder
    (Fig.~\ref{fig:deficit-decay}) whose declared secant slope is the frozen deficit-decay readout,
    and memory-dimension profiles. Run-only readouts; no functional form is fitted anywhere.
  \item \textbf{F3, pre-registration.} 338 prediction cells: 42 interval predictions, 2
    order-relation predictions, and 294 cells explicitly declared \texttt{no\_prediction}. The
    predictions registry pins the hashes of the frozen readouts, the East reference and the
    scoring script. The scored predictions concern the three hump readouts ($t_K$,
    $t_{\mathrm{peak}}$, height); the memory-dimension and deficit-decay readouts are not
    transferred. East intervals are rescaled to each target by a declared rule that uses the
    target kernel's spectral calibration, never its hump outcomes.
  \item \textbf{F4, target runs.} The FA and trap families are run against the registered
    predictions.
  \item \textbf{F5, mechanical scoring.} The frozen scorer (hash-pinned at F3, verified unchanged)
    compares predictions with observations, with no post-hoc widening or reinterpretation.
\end{itemize}

What the hashes do and do not establish should be stated plainly. They bind the scored
predictions to the exact bytes of the readouts, the reference and the scorer, so no prediction or
scoring rule can have been altered without detection. They do not, by themselves, certify the
chronological order of the stages, and the readouts registry's own freeze note records that the
planned per-stage commits were not made. ``Run-only'' is a procedural grade: no
same-present, different-readout witness is claimed for each individual GT7 readout. (GT3's
non-definability result concerns its own witness pair and the energy lens; it is not extended to
the GT7 readouts.)

\subsection{The scoreboard, unedited}

\claimref{NEG.gt7\_transfer\_failures}{37ac2b8f4bcb55cf1f2cf9f0518850a84039c3e51177d3000d0244b5ed6cc96e}
Of the 44 committed predictions, 22 were scored against observed target cells: \textbf{8 passed
and 14 failed}. The other 22 targeted cells that produced no observation. All 338 verdicts are
filed unedited (Fig.~\ref{fig:scoreboard}). The failure pattern is itself a filed, tested fact:

\begin{itemize}
  \item All 14 failures are FA-family interval predictions (5 $t_K$, 4 $t_{\mathrm{peak}}$,
    5 hump height), and every one is \emph{one-sided high}: the observed FA value exceeds the
    predicted upper bound. FA humps are systematically later and larger than the East-anchored
    intervals allowed.
  \item Failures cluster at the deeper intermediate temperature: 12 of 14 failing cells have
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mid}} = 2/5$, where every scored cell failed. At
    $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mid}} = 1/2$, 7 of 9 scored interval cells passed (plus the passing
    order-relation prediction), and the two misses there are borderline (hump height $0.2033$
    against a bound of $0.2008$; $t_K$ of $5$ against $[1,3]$).
  \item All 22 unobserved cells are trap-family. The trap target's exact hump search returned
    \texttt{found = false} in all 56 surface cells, with the uniform miss reason ``does not start
    below equilibrium.'' This is the dynamical corollary of the $\epsilon$-invariance lemma of
    \S\ref{sec:models}: the declared trap family's equilibrium does not move with $\epsilon$, so a
    temperature jump from equilibrium never leaves it below its new equilibrium, and the Kovacs
    protocol cannot produce a hump there at all.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reading the failures}

The failures are first-class results: the protocol is designed to be falsifiable, and it was.
Three readings are compatible with the data, and the protocol forbids choosing among them after the
fact. (a) East-to-FA transfer of run-only constants degrades with quench depth, in the direction
of FA responding slower and larger. (b) The declared rule for rescaling East intervals to FA,
rather than the readouts themselves, is what failed. (c) The trap column is not a partial failure
but a structural mismatch between the protocol and the declared trap family, explained after the
fact by the $\epsilon$-invariance lemma. Under any reading, the readouts are what the run-only
thesis says they are: readouts of runs, frozen, transferred and scored, not fitted. Score counts
are not a fitted scaling law, and no post-hoc reinterpretation of failed intervals is permitted or
performed.
