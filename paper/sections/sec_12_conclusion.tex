\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On the exact-finite class built here, the observable one usually monitors for a glass does not form
a closed description, and that failure is carried by certificates rather than intuitions. The
closure deficit exceeds its nonzero equilibrium baseline by a margin proved in exact arithmetic.
An exact Kovacs pair has the same present mean energy and different futures. Every predicate the
energy lens can define agrees on a pair that one structural coordinate separates. Two randomized
stops of one aging run share their entire energy distribution and still separate at every horizon
of a $21$-step window, so no descriptor of the energy law, fictive-temperature-like or otherwise,
predicts the future there. A kill-test panel rejects its own constructed look-alikes, one added
coordinate repairs each exhibited pair, and a registered transfer scoreboard is filed with its
failures intact. Just as important are the results that do not hold: the fixed-point route to a
fuller strict-extension filing fails on this shell, and an infinite-time conditional pressure gap
is provably zero. Around the results sits the part we consider most transferable: a claims ledger
tying every claim to hash-pinned evidence, the bindings machine-checked at build time, the wording
human-audited and independently reviewed, and a nonclaims ledger printed in full.

The field kept asking ``what is the order parameter of glass?'' On the class where we can afford
certificates, the answer is that the question is asked at the wrong level of description: the
declared energy readout, down to its full distribution, cannot express the distinction that the
witness pairs' futures carry. The Kovacs effect has been such a witness pair, sitting in the
literature since 1963. Whether that answer survives at larger sizes, richer observable panels and,
ultimately, in the laboratory is what the gap register and the bridge records are built to test
next.
