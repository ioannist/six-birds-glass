\section{GT2 and GT5: The Kovacs Witness and What It Costs to Repair}
\label{sec:gt2-gt5}

\subsection{The Kovacs protocol on the audited class}

The Kovacs protocol \cite{kovacs-1963} is: equilibrate hot, quench cold, age for $t_w$, jump up to
an intermediate temperature, and watch a one-time observable relax. We located it on East by an
exact search, run before any certificate and filed as a pre-certificate control. At $N=10$, with
$\epsilon: 7/10 \to 1/10 \to 2/5$ and $t_w = 40$, the mean energy after the second jump starts
below its new equilibrium value $20/7 \approx 2.857$ (at $\approx 2.741$), crosses it between
steps $1$ and $2$ ($\approx 2.803$ and $\approx 2.859$), and overshoots through a hump peaking at
$t_{\mathrm{peak}} = 35$ before returning (Fig.~\ref{fig:kovacs-hump}), all in exact rational
arithmetic. We write $t_K = 1$ for the last step before the crossing. The raw trace never equals
$20/7$ at an integer step; the preparation that does, a randomized stop between steps $1$ and $2$,
is constructed in \S\ref{sec:gt2-witness}.

The non-monotone excursion after the crossing is the classic signature that the present value
does not determine the future. For a finite Markov chain in the linear-response regime, Prados and
Brey showed that it is generic whenever the observable's relaxation is not a single exponential
\cite{prados-brey-2010}. That East- and FA-type models show a Kovacs hump was established
numerically by \cite{buhot-2003,arenzon-sellitto-2004}; what is new here is the exact-rational
trace and the certificate built on it.

\subsection{GT2: the witness certificate}
\label{sec:gt2-witness}

In plain terms: we exhibit two preparation histories of the East chain with exactly the same
present mean energy and provably different mean energies five steps later.

The first history is the Kovacs run stopped at the crossing. Since no single time step lands
exactly on $20/7$, the run is stopped at step $1$ with probability $\theta \approx 0.0359$ and at
step $2$ otherwise, with $\theta$ solved exactly so that the mixture's mean energy is $20/7$ (a
``randomized stop'', a declared move of the pipeline). The second history is the equilibrium
distribution at $\epsilon = 2/5$, whose mean energy is $20/7$ by definition. Both are then held at
$\epsilon = 2/5$ for five steps. The equilibrium history stays at $20/7 \approx 2.857$; the Kovacs
history moves to $\approx 3.058$, a gap of $\approx 0.2005$. Only the mean energy is matched at the
start, not the full energy distribution, and that is exactly what this lens sees. (GT6 removes
this limitation: there, the whole distribution is matched.) Precisely:

\claimref{GT2}{321f9bffdbf1368ec764a8fcde97d569dd9f4292ee5a98a0b6f30ac9483ce7bf}
On the declared scope (East, $N=10$, \texttt{kovacs\_moment} lens, declared $\epsilon$ grid and
continuation catalog), one exact-rational split pair of preparation histories is exhibited: the
two histories agree on the present lens value (mean energy $20/7$) and their futures differ, with
a strictly positive exact rational maximum future gap ($\approx 0.2005$ on the five-step hold; the
exact fraction is in the artifact). By \sbtcite{sbt-paper-a} Thm.~3.5, such a pair exists if and
only if the comparison map $\pi: M \to Q$ is strictly refining: the predictive description is
genuinely finer than the present one. The exported rational table of this pair is also checked in
Lean (\S\ref{sec:lean}). Grade: theorem (audited class).

A ``different future'' could mean three things, and we claim only the first: that the future
depends on more than the present readout. We do \emph{not} claim that the two histories took
different routes to the same state (a P3 route mismatch), and we claim no directionality or
irreversibility. In the framework's vocabulary the claim is \textbf{P4 staged-dependence}, filed on
the standard witness template (F-II \texttt{prop:p1-witness}). No P6\_drive audit was performed,
so no arrow-of-time statement of any kind is made on GT2's behalf.

\claimref{NEG.gt2\_loop\_certificate\_not\_built}{321f9bffdbf1368ec764a8fcde97d569dd9f4292ee5a98a0b6f30ac9483ce7bf}
A companion certificate about thermal loops was also planned: a closed temperature cycle that acts
trivially on the present description $Q$ but nontrivially on the predictive description $M$. It
is not filed. A tuned real East loop does return the current mean exactly, but the declared family
of histories it generates does not close within six rounds of iteration (it reaches $14$
histories), so no action of the loop on the quotients is certified. A constant-temperature
control loop scores zero on both quotients, as it must. The split-pair witness stands alone, and
nothing downstream depends on the loop.

\subsection{GT5: what it costs to buy the distinction back}

In plain terms: adding a single scalar to the energy readout is enough to tell the two GT2
histories apart, and a fictive-temperature-like index is one of three declared choices that work.
Precisely:

\claimref{GT5}{da6ecf8908baf0a2d65e677113882e4ec7e9fbc359d7c96e269e0c16f798de84}
On the declared scope (East, $N=10$, \texttt{kovacs\_moment} interface, the declared two-history
class), the present lens collapses the witness pair into one class ($|Q| = 1$ on the pair) while
the predictive quotient keeps two ($|M| = 2$), so the largest fiber of $\pi$ has size
$\mathrm{MaxFiber} = 2$, meeting the nontriviality threshold. The buyback curve
(Fig.~\ref{fig:buyback}) is published as a lift ledger per F-II \texttt{prop:forgetting-lifts}:
each added memory coordinate is a recorded, selected, non-unique lift. At budget $0$ the pair is
unresolved. At budget $1$, \emph{each} of the three declared scalar coordinates already resolves
it exactly (loss $0$), so the shipped witness saturates at $\texttt{saturation\_budget} = 1$.
Grade: theorem (audited class).

The three coordinates are expectations, over the history's microstate distribution $\mu$, of a
per-microstate quantity:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $T_f(\mu) = \mathbb{E}_\mu[\,i^*(E(n))\,]$, where $i^*(E)$ is the index of the grid value
    $\epsilon_i$ whose equilibrium mean energy is closest to $E$. This is an index into the
    declared grid, not a temperature. It is computed microstate by microstate, not from the
    history's mean energy, so two histories with equal mean energy can have different $T_f$.
  \item $D(\mu)$, the expected number of adjacent up-up spin pairs.
  \item $n_1(\mu)$, the expected occupation of the site next to the wall.
\end{itemize}
The loss is the sum of the two histories' squared errors when both are predicted by their common
mean, with unit weights; it is an in-sample bookkeeping quantity, not a held-out generalization
error.

The comparison with the GT6 pair (\S\ref{sec:gt6}) is instructive. $T_f$ depends on the history
only through its energy distribution. On the GT2 pair only the mean energy is matched, so $T_f$
can and does separate it. On the GT6 pair the entire energy distribution is matched, so the $T_f$
difference is exactly $0$ there, as it must be for any descriptor computed from the energy law,
while $D$ or $n_1$ alone still resolves that pair at budget $1$.

The translation to the glass literature is deliberately modest. The TNM fictive temperature
\cite{tool-1946,narayanaswamy-1971,moynihan-1976,hodge-1994} is the motivating analogy for $T_f$:
an energy-indexed effective temperature, added as a declared step from the present description
toward the predictive one. The implemented $T_f$ is the grid-index construction above, not the
TNM quantity itself. Modern treatments of the Kovacs effect likewise pair an effective
temperature with a second structural variable \cite{bouchbinder-langer-2010}, and the
multiparameter models the Kovacs literature moved to \cite{kahr-1979} are why the general question
is left open here rather than answered on two pairs. The certificate demonstrates the repair
\emph{mechanism} and its ledger discipline. MaxFiber $=2$ does not bound how many real-valued
coordinates memory needs in general, and we do not claim that a single fictive-temperature-like
scalar is insufficient in general.
