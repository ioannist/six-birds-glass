\subsection{GT3: the order-parameter search, foreclosed on its declared class}

In plain terms: a static order parameter built from the energy readout is a function of the energy
value. On $N=8$ spins the energy takes $9$ values, so there are exactly $2^9 = 512$ yes/no
functions of it, and we can check them all. Every one of them agrees on a pair of histories that a
finer readout of the same microstate, the occupation of the site next to the wall, tells apart.
Precisely:

\claimref{GT3}{25e78910189098978f58f5e083cd3553f42fd9eb2b94cc7818d0ed80f0f661a7}
On the declared scope (East, $N=8$, $\epsilon = 1/2$), the definability bound of F-III T14 makes
the static-order-parameter search space finite: a fixed lens with image size $9$ defines exactly
$2^9 = 512$ predicates. The sweep checks all $512$. Every one agrees on the exhibited witness
pair, while a readout based on $n_1$ (the occupation of the site next to the wall) separates it,
with exact gap $169/1536$ under the identity continuation (Table~\ref{tab:gt3-foreclosure}). The
witness pair is a standalone reflection pair constructed for this sweep and deliberately
\emph{not} chained to the GT2 certificate, so the two results fail independently. The foreclosure
is constructive: the distinguishing readout is exhibited and proven absent from the set of
energy-definable predicates, not inferred from counting. Grade: theorem (audited class).

The field-facing sentence ``there is no static order parameter for glass'' is licensed here
\emph{only} as the declared-class shadow of this theorem (F-II item E2), with the class, the
catalog and the suppressed content named in the same breath: no predicate definable from the
declared energy lens on the declared class separates what memory separates. Reading it as a
statement about other instruments, other lens classes (in particular the amorphous order of
random first-order transition theory \cite{kirkpatrick-thirumalai-wolynes-1989,lubchenko-wolynes-2007},
the configurational-entropy line descending from \cite{adam-gibbs-1965}, or the local-structure
measures surveyed in \cite{royall-williams-2015}), or the continuum is not licensed, and this
paper does not do so.

What the theorem does license, via the framework's no-free-distinction law (F-IV F12), is narrower
and exact: any readout that \emph{does} separate this pair must carry information beyond the
energy readout. Here that information is a present structural coordinate of the microstate
($n_1$, under the identity continuation), not a record of the history. The separation shows that
the energy-based description is incomplete. In fact the two histories have the same complete
energy distribution, so every descriptor computed from it, not only the $512$ predicates, agrees
on them. GT6 (\S\ref{sec:gt6}) supplies the same full-law match for two preparations of a real
aging run, separated by the East dynamics rather than by a present readout, and GT5 shows that
one added coordinate suffices to repair the Kovacs pair.

\subsection{GT4: the kill-test panel}

A memory signature can be faked. GT4 builds five schematic fixtures for the ways it can be faked
and checks that the same deterministic classifier that accepts the Kovacs candidate rejects all
five. The candidate, \texttt{kovacs\_wheel}, is a reflection pair at $N=8$ that the full spin panel
distinguishes but its reflection-invariant part does not. The control fixtures use the 16-state $N=4$ carrier and two or three
interfaces, with declared readouts and continuations; they are regime schemata, structural toys,
not physical mechanisms:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{protocol trap}: a pair whose readout difference is a residue of its schedule.
    Its cleared twin exposes the toy clock label in the current readout while leaving every
    future unchanged, so the pair is visibly different now and the apparent memory disappears.
  \item The \emph{flattenable} pair: an apparent memory caused by an incomplete continuation
    catalog. Its cleared twin applies an admissible completion that averages the declared
    two-state orbit, an idempotent stochastic map on the same support, after which the futures
    agree.
  \item The \emph{explicitly latent} pair differs in a bit that the present readout does not see.
  \item The \emph{dissipative} fixture merges the two histories at a later interface.
  \item The \emph{flat} control is two copies of the $N=4$ equilibrium distribution, which by
    construction has nothing to separate.
\end{itemize}
Precisely:

\claimref{GT4}{b9ff2b977e8e947c9138d4d60a4c9588fa8dbfacd6dc902a97249978d40fd6d5}
Over the declared $9$-member benchmark family of small structural packages, the deterministic
exact triage classification identifies \texttt{kovacs\_wheel}, the Kovacs memory candidate, as
the sole \texttt{coherent\_candidate}, while the five declared non-coherent regime controls
(\texttt{artifact\_trap}, \texttt{dissipative}, \texttt{explicit\_latent}, \texttt{flat},
\texttt{flattenable}) are correctly killed or cleared (Table~\ref{tab:gt4-regime-triage}).
Protocol-trap discipline is applied: raw and cleared classifications are both recorded, so the
table shows each artifact being caught. For instance, the naive protocol-trap fixture classifies
as \texttt{artifact\_trap} raw, and its clock-exposing twin as \texttt{flat}. Grade: theorem
(audited class), over the declared finite family only, not a shell-general theorem. The
robustness fractions cover three declared structural perturbations; they are not probabilistic
confidence guarantees.

\subsection{GB1: the protocol-trap caveat, carried openly}

The ``protocol trap'' is a warning about arrows of time, not about memory. A system whose dynamics
switch between several kernels under a clock can show a spurious time asymmetry if the clock is
left outside the state. The framework's published theorem on this (F-I T-AOT-02) concerns the
random-scan autonomous lift initialized at its stationary product law $\pi\times s$: when the
clock is reversible and every phase kernel is reversible with respect to a \emph{common}
measure $\pi$, that lift, started stationary, has zero entropy production and zero
path-reversal asymmetry at every horizon, so any observed arrow must come from an external schedule, a non-stationary
start, or a stroboscopic fit. The conclusion does not cover arbitrary initial laws. Kovacs kernels $K_\epsilon$ are reversible with respect to a different Gibbs
measure for each $\epsilon$, so the theorem does not apply as published: the temperature switches
contribute a boundary term.

\claimref{GB1}{4173705a52c358bc74b51815929cb2a28c8f680b3421dafdb7633e97b051fa8e}
GB1 is the temperature-protocol version of that audit, in the style of the protocol-trap
microarticle \sbtcite{sbt-protocol-trap}, at recognition grade. Three facts are established. (i) An
exact identity expresses the stationary entropy production through a candidate measure built from
the phase Gibbs laws plus a boundary term. (ii) That candidate expression is \emph{not} a general
upper bound: an exact rational three-state counterexample, with a reversible clock and a common
energy function, exceeds it by more than $9.5\times10^{-8}$, certified with rational logarithm
bounds. (iii) A corrected stationary budget is proved: it adds an explicit residual,
$\operatorname{osc}(b)\,k\,\mathrm{TV}(\hat\mu P,\hat\mu)/(1-D_k)$, controlled by a Dobrushin
contraction coefficient $D_k<1$, and it recovers the published zero-entropy-production conclusion
when all phases share one Gibbs law and the clock is reversible. No bound of this form holds for
arbitrary initial laws; a two-state transient counterexample shows why. The three-state
demonstration in the artifact is a float64 numerical check.

GB1's role is to keep the arrow-of-time audit separate from the memory classification. GT4's triage
computation is deterministic and exact, but the clock-audit \emph{discipline} it applies (raw
versus cleared classification) rests, for temperature protocols, on a recognition-grade
demonstration with a stationary budget rather than on a closed transient theorem. This is the
framework's conditional-reporting rule working as designed.
